## Cherry Magic is the Best BL I have Watched Yet

*Starring: Tay and Newwie*
### Introduction

I’ve recently hyperfixated on what series to watch next, and it made me realize I never really talked about *Cherry Magic*. It’s easily the best BL (Boys’ Love) series I’ve ever watched — a show that’s incredibly comforting, gentle, and warm.

### What I Loved

What stood out the most was how *Cherry Magic* avoids the typical “ragebait” characters. Instead, it simply focuses on the couple navigating life and figuring things out together. There are no explosive conflicts, yet the story remains engaging because of how thoughtfully it handles misunderstandings. Both characters are exceptionally understanding of each other, which makes everything feel light and satisfying to watch.

### Comparisons to Other BLs

Generally, I don’t like how many BL series resort to ragebait or conflicts that feel forced and poorly justified. That said, there are still several that I really enjoyed:

- *Dark Blue Kiss (TayNew)*
- *Cooking Crush (OffGun)*
- *Bad Buddy (OhmNanon)*
- *Sweet Tooth Good Dentist (MarkOhm)*
- *A Boss and A Babe (ForceBook)*

But *Cherry Magic* is the only BL I’ve watched so far where none of the episodes ever felt off. (I almost included *Never Let Me Go (PondPhuwin)* in this list, but I honestly can’t stand Phuwin’s character, despite understanding he’s a nepo baby — it was just too much for me.)

### Where Cherry Magic Stands Out

All of the shows I mentioned have strong stories, especially when you compare them to GMMTV’s usual track record of hit-or-miss BLs. Still, *Cherry Magic* never had a single episode where I thought, “What were they trying to do with this?”  

Other series often include entire episodes centered on conflicts that could have easily been avoided, or episodes that don’t meaningfully progress the characters (either individually or as a couple). Meanwhile, *Cherry Magic* made sure every episode moved Achi and Karan’s story forward. It’s why I’d recommend it wholeheartedly.

### A Small Side Note on ThamePo

As a side note, I still love *ThamePo (WilliamEst)*, but I’m not fully sold on the story. I honestly refuse to believe episodes 10-12 even exist — the story already felt complete after episode 9. Rewatching it made me notice so many plot holes and how lacking ThamePo’s character development was. I just hope to see more of William and Est, since they’re both genuinely good actors.

### Conclusion

Overall, *Cherry Magic* is a standout for me. It’s rare to find a series that forgoes cheap dramatic devices for something that feels real, gentle, and meaningful. It’s the kind of show where every episode earns its place, and that’s something truly special.
